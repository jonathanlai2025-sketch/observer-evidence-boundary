# Concept

A representation of reality is not reality.

A computer can say “payment completed,” “account deleted,” “access revoked,” or “action successful.” Saying it does not necessarily mean the real-world outcome occurred.

Observer exists to keep that distinction visible after an important action is claimed.

## Distinctions

These are different things:

- Claim ≠ evidence
- Evidence ≠ authority
- Authority ≠ execution
- Execution ≠ outcome
- Receipt ≠ reality
- Representation ≠ the thing represented

AI capability may increase without automatically increasing authority or evidentiary standing.

Intelligence is not permission.
Repetition is not corroboration.
A screenshot is not settlement.
A successful API call is not independent proof that the intended outcome occurred.

## The loop

At a high level:

```
Reality
   → Perception
   → Representation
   → Cognition
   → Governed consequence
   → Reality
   → Observe again
```

Inbound: what are we justified in believing?
Outbound: what are we permitted to cause?
Afterward: what does independent evidence actually establish?

Observer is the afterward. It assesses claimed outcomes against qualifying evidence. It does not perform the action, grant the permission, or substitute the actor’s story for the world.

## Uncertainty is a first-class result

UNKNOWN is not a failed assessment.

If qualifying independent evidence is absent, the honest result remains UNKNOWN — even if the agent is confident, several systems agree, a screenshot looks perfect, or someone needs a yes-or-no answer.

An organization may still choose, as policy, to wait, escalate, or proceed while accepting risk. That policy choice does not rewrite UNKNOWN into CONFIRMED.
